Whistle: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Whistle: Referee Career ("Whistle", "the app") is a single-player football referee game.

Summary

Whistle has no accounts of its own and never asks for your name, email or phone number. It shows ads through Google AdMob, sells optional in-app items through Google Play, can keep your career in your Google Play Games account, counts how the game is played with Google Analytics for Firebase, and tells Meta (Facebook) when the app is installed and opened so we can measure our own ads. These services handle some data, as described below. We never receive your career.

What stays on your device

Your career save, settings, purchases record and any data packs you install are stored on your device. If Android backup is on for your Google account, Android also copies them to your own encrypted Google backup and puts them back when you install Whistle again; you can turn this off in your phone's backup settings. We never receive this data and cannot see or recover it.

Cloud save (Google Play Games)

If you are signed in to Google Play Games, the app keeps a copy of your career in your own Google Play Games account (saved games), so it comes back on a new phone. Only the game's own save is stored there. Google Play Games' terms and Google's privacy policy apply: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Ads (Google AdMob)

The free game shows ads: a banner, an optional video you choose to watch for an in-game reward, a full-screen ad between some matches and a sponsored post in the in-game phone feed. Ads are provided by Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (used for approximate location), device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google uses this under its own policy: https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the app asks for your consent before personalised ads are shown, using Google's consent form. You can change your choice at any time in Settings > Ad privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. Buying "Remove ads" turns off full-screen ads.

Game statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To improve the game, the app counts how it is played: for example when a career starts, when a match starts and ends, the assessor's mark and how far the career has got. These counts are sent to Google Analytics for Firebase with a random app-instance ID, device and app information and approximate location (from the IP address). They never include the name you give your referee or anything you type. Google processes them for us: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

Install and app events (Meta)

We advertise Whistle on Facebook and Instagram. To know which of our ads lead to people installing and playing the game, the app uses Meta's app events (Facebook SDK). Where the law requires consent, this only runs if you allowed storing and reading information on your device in the consent form; if you did not, nothing is sent to Meta.

When it runs, the app tells Meta that it was installed and opened, and about a few moments in the game: a career started, the first match finished, a match finished (its number and level), a promotion, and coming back the day after you first played and a week later. With these, Meta also receives your device's advertising ID, a random app ID, device and app information and your IP address. Meta uses this to measure and improve our ads, and may use it to show our ads to people like our players or to remind past players of the game. In US states with their own privacy laws, the app asks Meta to limit its use of this data (Limited Data Use). Meta's privacy policy applies: https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy.

You can stop this at any time: change your choice in Settings > Ad privacy choices (where the form is offered), or reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. You can also manage how Meta uses information about your activity in other apps in your Facebook or Instagram settings under Accounts Center > Your information and permissions > Your activity off Meta technologies.

Sharing

When you tap a share button, the app makes a picture of your match report or a short video of a big call and hands it to the app you pick (the Android share sheet, or the Facebook or Instagram app if you tap their button). Nothing is posted unless you post it yourself in that app. To show the Facebook and Instagram buttons, the app checks whether those two apps are installed; it checks no other apps.

Purchases (Google Play)

In-app purchases are processed by Google Play. We never see your card or payment details. Google tells the app which items you own, so it can unlock them; we do not receive your name or email. Google Play's privacy policy applies: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Network use

Apart from the services above, the app works offline. It also connects to the internet when you choose to install a data pack from a web link you type in. The app downloads that file and sends nothing about you with the request. The site hosting the file may see your IP address, as with any web request, under its own privacy policy.

Permissions

The Android app uses vibration (for the whistle and match feedback), internet access (ads, purchases, cloud save, statistics, app events and data packs) and the advertising ID (ads and app events). It does not use your microphone, camera, contacts, precise location or storage.

Keeping and deleting data

We do not hold your data ourselves. Google keeps Firebase statistics for at most 14 months. Meta keeps app events under its own policy. To have data deleted, reset your advertising ID in your Android settings, uninstall the app, or contact us at the email below; we will pass deletion requests on to Google and Meta for the IDs you give us.

Children

Whistle is not directed at children under 13. Its ads are not personalised for users who say they are under the age of consent in their region.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this same address with a new date.

Contact

Questions about this policy go to the developer email shown on the app's Google Play page.
